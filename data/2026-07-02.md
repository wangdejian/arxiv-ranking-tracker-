### [2] [Why Advanced Encoders Lag on Sparse Retrieval? The Answer and an Approach to Bridging Vocabulary Gaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00004)
*Zhichao Geng, Yang Yang*

Main category: cs.IR | Domain: 召回模型/Recall | Importance: 高/High

TL;DR: 论文发现ModernBERT等先进模型在稠密检索上表现优异，但在学习型稀疏检索（LSR）上反而不如老旧的BERT-base。根源在于"词汇鸿沟"：现代分词器使用原始、大小写敏感的词汇表，将同一语义单元映射为冗余表面形式，浪费模型容量并阻碍词汇匹配。作者提出Vocabulary Transfer（VT）框架，通过语义初始化和激活势校准（APC）机制，将先进编码器迁移到稀疏友好、归一化的词汇表上，在BEIR基准上取得SOTA（52.4 nDCG，提升+4.7）。

Ranking Relevance: 该论文主要聚焦于检索（召回）阶段，特别是学习型稀疏检索（LSR）。虽然直接应用在召回环节，但稀疏检索在搜推广系统中常与排序模型协同工作——检索结果的质量直接影响排序模型的输入分布。此外，论文中讨论的"词汇鸿沟"问题、语义初始化、激活势校准等技术和理论分析（通过词汇粗粒化收紧泛化界）对排序模型中的特征工程、embedding初始化等也有借鉴意义。对于工业搜推广系统，该工作提供了一种低成本升级召回模型的方式，可间接提升整体排序效果。

<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 现代基础模型（如ModernBERT）在稠密检索中表现优异，但在学习型稀疏检索上意外地逊于BERT-base。作者识别出根本原因是词汇鸿沟：现代分词器的词汇表是为无损重建设计的，包含大量冗余表面形式（如大小写变体），这浪费了模型容量，干扰了稀疏检索中关键的词汇匹配信号。

Method: 提出Vocabulary Transfer（VT）框架，包含两个核心组件：1）语义初始化（Semantic Initialization）：利用嵌入空间的几何拓扑结构，从源词汇表映射到目标（稀疏友好、归一化）词汇表，保留语义结构；2）激活势校准（Activation Potential Calibration, APC）：对齐预训练流形与稀疏约束，防止微调中出现的死神经元和稠密塌陷问题。该框架模型无关，计算开销小。

Result: 在BEIR基准上，VT使ModernBERT达到52.4 nDCG（+4.7提升），超过所有LSR方法；RoBERTa-large等本不适用于LSR的模型也被"复活"；方法还泛化到免推理架构和专业领域。代码和模型已开源。

Conclusion: 先进模型在LSR上的性能滞后并非架构缺陷，而是可解决的词汇不匹配问题。VT框架提供了一种低成本、模型无关的解决方案，为稀疏检索领域释放了现代基础模型的潜力。

Abstract: While advanced foundation models like ModernBERT significantly outperform older architectures in dense retrieval, they surprisingly lag behind the aging BERT-base baseline in learned sparse retrieval (LSR). We identify the root cause as the \textit{Vocabulary Gap}: modern tokenizers utilize raw, case-sensitive vocabularies designed for lossless reconstruction, which map single semantic units to redundant surface forms, wasting model capacity on morphological noise and hindering lexical matching. We formalize this intuition through a theoretical framework, demonstrating that appropriate vocabulary coarse-graining can tighten the generalization bounds by reducing complexity of the hypothesis class, provided that semantic integrity is preserved. To resolve this, we propose \textbf{Vocabulary Transfer (VT)}, a model-agnostic framework that migrates advanced encoders to sparse-friendly, normalized vocabularies with minimal computational cost.
  VT utilizes a novel \textbf{Semantic Initialization} via spatial topology to preserve geometric structure and an \textbf{Activation Potential Calibration (APC)} mechanism to align pre-trained manifolds with sparsity constraints, preventing the dead neuron and dense collapse observed in standard fine-tuning. Empirically, VT is universally effective: it enables ModernBERT to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the BEIR benchmark (\textbf{52.4} nDCG, a \textbf{+4.7} improvement), resuscitates failing models like RoBERTa-large, and generalizes seamlessly to inference-free architectures and specialized domains. These results confirm that the performance lag is not an architectural deficiency but a solvable vocabulary mismatch. We've released our code and models.\footnote{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/vocab-transfer/. All details included.}
